# Innovation and partnering section: rules

Each paper with a plausible commercial or partnering angle is scored on four fixed checks. Claude answers each one with evidence; the script, not Claude, decides the outcome.

## The four checks

1. **Market.** There is an existing market or identifiable buyers for this result today, not "could be useful someday". Pass needs a named product category or customer type that already spends money on this problem.
2. **Partner.** At least one specific company is active in the space and verified by web search as real and currently operating. Prefer Ottawa, then Canadian, then international. Small to large companies all count. A government lab or agency can be named as a second partner but does not pass this check on its own.
3. **Carleton's role.** A Carleton Faculty of Science member is first, last or corresponding author, or the abstract makes clear the core contribution came from their group. A Carleton middle author on another institution's work fails.
4. **Evidence.** The paper shows enough for a company to engage: a working method or prototype with reported performance, or validation beyond a single cell line or lab-scale proof of concept.

## Outcome (applied by the script)

- **Act on:** all four checks pass.
- **Worth a conversation:** market and partner pass, but Carleton's role or evidence fails. The digest says what holds it back.
- **Not yet:** market or partner fails. Listed in one line with the reason.

## Also state

- **Route:** licence or partnership. If code or methods are already public (GitHub, open preprint), it is a partnership or know-how play, not a licence. Mention patent grace periods only when relevant (Canada and the US allow 12 months after the inventor's own disclosure; most of Europe allows none).
- **One concrete next step:** who to call and which program fits (for example Mitacs Accelerate, NSERC Alliance, OCI, NRC IRAP for the company). Flag co-owners at other institutions.
- Empty weeks are expected. Never pad.
