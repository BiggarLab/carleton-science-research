Steven Cooke Biology, Environmental & Interdisciplinary Sci

Cooke: telemetry-based fish monitoring for hydro operators and fisheries consultants

cited by BC Hydro (Canada), CHN Energy (China), China Three Gorges Corporation (China) co-authored with BC Hydro (Canada), InStream Fisheries Research (Canada), Kintama (Canada) Fonds de recherche du Québec – Nature et technologies, Mitacs preprint, patent clock to 2027-06-15 129 papers in 3 years, 63 in the last 2

What. Cooke's group runs acoustic telemetry and tagging studies on fish survival, movement and habitat use. Recent work includes a performance test of a 307 kHz telemetry system in a small freshwater lake, post-release survival of angled Brook Trout, and Walleye spawning habitat selection in Hamilton Harbour. Hydro utilities already co-author and fund this work.

Market. Buyers are hydro operators and the consultancies they hire for fish passage, survival and habitat studies. <cite index="26-9">Innovasea's V3 tag operates at 307 kHz,</cite> the same band as the Carleton lake test. I have not confirmed that the paper used Innovasea equipment.

Who to approach.

  • Kintama Research Services (Nanaimo, BC). Designs and runs large acoustic telemetry arrays and already co-authors with Cooke. It is a small firm (11-50 staff), which suits Mitacs.
  • InStream Fisheries Research (Vancouver and Squamish, BC). Provides telemetry, fish counters and population assessment to government, industry and First Nations clients. It co-authors with Cooke.
  • Innovasea Systems (Boston, US (acoustic telemetry line includes the former Vemco and HTI)). Added as the one new company. It makes acoustic receivers and tags for fish passage and fine-scale tracking. <cite index="22-5">It became the leader in acoustic telemetry tools after acquiring Vemco and HTI in 2019.</cite>

Next step. Call Cooke first. Ask which of the 307 kHz, hypoxia and release-survival results a client has asked to repeat, and who the non-Carleton co-authors are. Then propose a Mitacs Accelerate project with InStream or Kintama, with NRC IRAP as the route for the company's own costs. The existing NSERC Alliance agreements with BC Hydro, OPG and other partners may set IP and publication terms, so have the research office read them before anything is offered. Hydro partners and any co-authors at other institutions are potential co-owners.

Route. Partnership. The value is field methods and know-how for fish tagging and tracking studies, not a protectable device. The 307 kHz paper is an open preprint dated 2026-06-15. In Canada and the US the 12-month grace period runs to 2027-06-15. Most of Europe has no grace period, so any filing right there is already gone. No Carleton patents are on record.

Seán Barry Chemistry

Barry: ALD precursors and area-selective deposition for chip makers

cited by Adeka (Japan), Tokyo Electron Technology Solutions Limited (Japan) co-authored with Solvay (United States) Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada CRDPJ 543877-19, Semiconductor Research Corporation 8 papers in 3 years, 3 in the last 2

What. Barry's group develops precursor chemistry and deposition processes for atomic layer deposition. Recent work covers molybdenum carbide films, precursor decomposition on surfaces, and carbene layers for area-selective deposition.

Market. Precursor suppliers and chip makers pay for new precursors and process chemistry, and Adeka and Tokyo Electron already cite the work. <cite index="63-3">Entegris sells organosilane precursors and molecules for area-selective deposition,</cite> which overlaps with Barry's topics.

Who to approach.

  • Solvay (United States (co-author); NSERC partner record lists Cytec Canada and Solvay Specialty Polymers). Co-authors with Barry and is tied to the CRD project. Its scope for deposition precursors is unconfirmed.
  • ADEKA (Tokyo, Japan). <cite index="38-2">Develops many ALD and CVD precursors as a leading supplier.</cite> It already cites the line.
  • Entegris (Billerica, Massachusetts, US). Added as the one new company. <cite index="63-2">It sells ultra-pure precursors for molybdenum and other metal films,</cite> which matches Barry's molybdenum carbide work.

Next step. Call Barry. Ask what the Solvay CRD (CRDPJ 543877-19) covers, whether it is still active, and whether any precursor or process is unpublished and worth a filing. Have the research office read the CRD agreement, because Solvay may co-own results and hold publication rights. If the terms allow it, pitch an NSERC Alliance or a follow-on CRD to ADEKA or Entegris, confirming eligibility with a Canadian-based partner or subsidiary. Tokyo Electron Technology Solutions has a Canadian link to check.

Route. Partnership. No Carleton patents are on record, no open preprints are flagged, and the published methods are already public. The CRD with Solvay probably sets IP terms. Treat this as a know-how and follow-on research play unless Barry has an unpublished precursor that merits a filing.
