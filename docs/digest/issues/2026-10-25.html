Biochemistry 1

Jenny Bruin Biochemistry, Amy Rand Chemistrycross-unit

PFOS Is Detected in Human Pancreas and Dysregulates Glucose-Stimulated Insulin Secretion across Model Systems

The team measured three PFAS chemicals in blood and pancreas samples from 88 human organ donors and found PFOS in 99% of the pancreas samples. They then tested PFOS on insulin-producing cells and islets from humans and mice, where prolonged high doses suppressed insulin release and acute high doses increased it in mouse islets and some human islets, which supports a direct effect on beta cells and shows that results depend on the model used.

Biology 2

Steven Cooke Biology

Energetic costs of catch and release angling in B rook T rout ( S alvelinus fontinalis ) at two temperatures

The researchers simulated catch and release angling on adult Brook Trout at 10°C and 15°C, measured how much oxygen the fish used to recover and how long they took to start feeding again, and used a model to predict effects on growth over 30 days. One simulated capture changed predicted growth by up to 4.1% and five captures by up to 21.0%, and the authors describe the energy costs of the moderate stressors tested as relatively small within the trout's optimal temperature range.

Chemistry 1

Daniel Grégoire Chemistry, Katherine Marczenko Chemistry, David McMullin Chemistry

Microbially‐Induced Corrosion of Cobalt by Pseudomonas chlororaphis subspecies aureofaciens Is Driven by the Complex Interplay Between Abiotic and Biotic Processes That Control Cobalt Solubility

The study tested whether chemicals called phenazines, made by the bacterium Pseudomonas chlororaphis, are what corrode metallic cobalt. Mutants that could not make phenazines corroded as much cobalt as normal cells, so the corrosion depends on a mix of chemical and biological processes that control how much cobalt dissolves, which matters for protecting infrastructure and for recovering cobalt from solid waste.

Computer Science 2

Edward Melcer Computer Science

Effectiveness of Game-Based Interventions: Risk Perception and Preparedness in a Wildfire-Prone University Setting

This work-in-progress paper surveyed 100 undergraduates about wildfire risk perception and ran a small lab study with 12 students comparing a game-based and a website-based learning tool. Students spent longer with the game, most did not make a Go-Bag afterward, and a majority in both groups talked with friends or family about wildfire risk.

Annual Symposium on Computer-Human Interaction in Play · Oct 22 · open access
Prosenjit Bose Computer Science

Computational aspects of disks enclosing many points

The paper gives algorithms that find a pair of points in a set on a plane such that any disk containing that pair must also contain a guaranteed fraction of all the points. It reports running times and guaranteed fractions for several versions of the problem, including a linear-time method that guarantees at least one third of the points when the points are in convex position.

Discrete Applied Mathematics · Oct 21 · open access

Neuroscience 8

Kim Hellemans Neuroscience

Do Words Matter? A Vignette Study Exploring How Substance Use Descriptions Influence Perceptions of the Substance Involved in Police Encounters

About 1,049 Canadian adults read a police-encounter scenario describing substance use in one of five ways and then chose the characteristics of the person described. Most chose alcohol, a White person and a male, while Black and Indigenous identities were about half as likely as White to be chosen in some conditions, which the authors read as an implicit link between these groups and problematic use.

Kim Hellemans Neuroscience

Understanding Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR) as a Mediator in the Perceived Stress and High-risk Cannabis Use Relationship

The study collected saliva samples across one day from 95 Carleton undergraduates to test whether the cortisol awakening response explains the link between perceived stress and high-risk cannabis use. Stress, depression and anxiety scores were positively related to cannabis use scores, but the cortisol awakening response was not significantly related to cannabis use and did not act as a mediator.

Carleton undergraduate journal of science. · Oct 22 · open access
Argel Aguilar Valles Neuroscience

Amphetamine-Induced Activation of Phospho-4EBP's and c-Fos in Adult Schizophrenia-Mutant Mice

The study gave amphetamine to mice carrying a schizophrenia-associated mutation in the 4E-BP2 gene and measured neuron activation markers and protein synthesis signalling in key brain regions. Male mutants had more DARPP-32 and c-Fos positive cells while females showed no difference, and the small signalling increase seen in normal mice was weaker in mutants, which suggests sex-specific effects.

Argel Aguilar Valles Neuroscience

Translational Control of the Acute Stress Response by eIF4E Binding Proteins

The study compared normal mice with mice lacking 4E-BP1 and 4E-BP2 in forebrain excitatory neurons, which raises protein synthesis, after 15 minutes of restraint stress, using self-grooming as the stress response. Normal males groomed more than the knockout males, females followed a similar pattern that was not statistically significant, and the authors conclude that increased translation in these neurons dampens the behavioural response to acute stress.

Carleton undergraduate journal of science. · Oct 22 · open access
Robyn McQuaid Neuroscience

Behavioural and Biological Predictors of Stress: Sleep, Growth Factors, and Sex

In 237 young adults, half of whom were exposed to a standard laboratory stress test, the researchers examined how sleep quality, two blood growth factors (BDNF and FGF-21) and sex relate to stress and anxiety. Poorer sleep went with higher perceived stress and anxiety, and tiredness was the strongest predictor of anxiety and fear responses to the stress test, with growth factor associations differing by sex.

Carleton undergraduate journal of science. · Oct 22 · open access
Natalina Salmaso Neuroscience

Exploring the effects of H₂O₂ on parkin activity in cellular models

The study exposed cultured cells to hydrogen peroxide to mimic oxidative stress and looked at what happens to parkin, a protein linked to early-onset Parkinson's disease. Parkin moved to different locations in the cell and its solubility changed with dose, which fits oxidative modification of the protein and gives a basis for further work.

Carleton undergraduate journal of science. · Oct 22 · open access
Matthew Holahan Neuroscience

Effects of a DNA Aptamer on α-Synuclein PFF–Induced Morphological Deficits in an in-vitro Model of Parkinson’s Disease

The study tested whether a DNA aptamer that blocks alpha-synuclein aggregation, a feature of Parkinson's disease, also protects the shape of cultured neuron-like cells exposed to toxic alpha-synuclein fibrils. The fibrils reduced cell body size and neurite length and the aptamer partly offset this, but none of the differences were statistically significant, so no clear protective effect was shown.

Argel Aguilar Valles Neuroscience

Translational control of microglial metabolism, inflammatory responses, and survival by the eIF4E-binding proteins

The researchers examined a protein called 4E-BP1 in the immune cells of the brain, called microglia, in Alzheimer's disease. Higher levels of the protein in cerebrospinal fluid strengthened the link between a marker called soluble TREM2 and neurodegeneration, and in mouse microglia, deleting 4E-BP1 and 4E-BP2 increased mitochondrial mass, reduced inflammatory signalling and protected cells from amyloid-induced death.

Physics 2

Jesse Heilman Physics, Thomas Koffas Physics with a large collaboration

Combination of ATLAS and CMS Searches for Higgs Boson Pair Production at $\sqrt{s}$=13 TeV

ATLAS and CMS combined their Run 2 searches for Higgs boson pair production, using data from 126 to 140 fb^-1. The combined result is a signal strength of 0.8 (+0.9, -0.7) relative to the Standard Model at 1.1 standard deviations, so it is not an observation, and it sets limits on how strongly the Higgs boson couples to itself.
