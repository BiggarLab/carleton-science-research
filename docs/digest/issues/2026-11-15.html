Biology 5

Steven Cooke Biology

Using short-term postrelease behavior to assess novel methods of facilitated recovery for angled Smallmouth Bass

Steven Cooke and colleagues put biologgers on Smallmouth Bass after simulated catch-and-release, including air exposure, and compared the fish's depth, temperature use and swimming over the first 12 minutes after release under different assisted recovery methods. Fish held 15 minutes in a flow chamber swam more like unexposed control fish than those exposed to air and released right away, so the authors conclude this technique can improve recovery of fish intended for release.

North American Journal of Fisheries Management · Nov 13 · open access
Christina Davy Biology

Genetic data used in the assessment of at‐risk species: A Canadian case study

Christina Davy and colleagues reviewed every status report that Canada's wildlife assessment committee (COSEWIC) produced from 1983 to 2022 to see how often genetic data were used in assessing species at risk. Genetic data appeared in 51% of assessed units and only 2.6% mentioned genetic monitoring, which the authors take as a sign that the genetic evidence base needs strengthening to support national and global biodiversity targets.

Conservation Science and Practice · Nov 11 · open access
Grégory Bulté Biology

Wild Northern Map Turtle (Graptemys geographica) can live at least half a century in Ontario

Grégory Bulté and colleagues used long-running capture and recapture records from Opinicon Lake, Ontario, including turtles marked in the 1970s, to estimate how long wild Northern Map Turtles live. One female marked in 1978 was recaptured in 2025 with an estimated age of 57 years, and 17 other turtles had minimum ages of 31 to 55 years, which the authors say matters for conservation of the species.

The Canadian Field-Naturalist · Nov 10 · open access
Rachel Buxton Biology

Relationships between GPS-derived outdoor activity space ambient heat exposure, mental health, and salivary cortisol in a longitudinal, repeated measures sample of adult detroiters

Rachel Buxton and colleagues tracked adults in 11 Detroit neighbourhoods with GPS devices over several years and linked their outdoor heat exposure to anxiety and depression symptoms and to salivary cortisol, a stress marker. Higher heat exposure was weakly associated with more anxiety and depression symptoms (not statistically significant at the usual threshold) and was significantly associated with a cortisol pattern indicating higher stress, while exposure to greenness did not change these results.

International Journal of Health Geographics · Nov 9 · open access
Steven Cooke Biology

Sub-ice thermal gradients strongly influence winter depth use of an iconic cold-water fish

Steven Cooke and colleagues used multiyear acoustic telemetry on Lake Trout in 11 lakes to see what controls the depths they use under winter ice, and found that the depth of the 2 °C water layer explains much of it. Applying a model to 877,927 lakes in Canada and Alaska, they predicted that 73% of the 66,500 known Lake Trout lakes have warmer winter conditions, where the fish would stay in the upper water column.

Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences · Nov 9 · open access

Chemistry 2

Apollinaire Tsopmo Chemistry

In silico and in vitro studies suggest anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory potential of Turbinaria ornata and Turbinaria decurrens

Apollinaire Tsopmo and colleagues analyzed the chemical makeup of extracts from two seaweeds common in Indonesian waters and tested them with computer modelling and lab cell assays for anti-inflammatory and immune-related activity. The results suggest the extracts and some of their compounds are candidates for further study, but the authors state that the docking scores do not show inhibition and that dose-response and animal work are still needed.

Scientific Reports · Nov 9 · open access
Tyler Avis Chemistry

Differential Activity of Bacillus velezensis Strains Producing Antimicrobial Lipopeptides on Mycelial Growth Inhibition of Pome Fruit Pathogens

Tyler Avis and colleagues tested three strains of the bacterium Bacillus velezensis against four fungal pathogens of apples and pears, and measured the antimicrobial lipopeptide compounds each strain produces. All strains reduced growth of three of the pathogens, and the type of lipopeptide recovered depended on the extraction method, so the authors advise caution when attributing ecological roles to these compounds.

Chemistry & Biodiversity · Nov 1 · open access

Fred Gaidies Earth Sciences

Pitfalls in metamorphic phase equilibria modelling caused by phase-in reaction kinetics: insights from overstepping the staurolite-forming reaction

No abstract was available, so this summary is based on the title only. The paper by Fred Gaidies appears to address pitfalls in modelling metamorphic phase equilibria when reaction kinetics delay the formation of new minerals, using overstepping of the staurolite-forming reaction as the example.

Physics 1

Dag Gillberg Physics, Jesse Heilman Physics, Alain Bellerive Physics, Thomas Koffas Physics, Manuella Vincter Physics with a large collaboration

Measurement of J/ψ-jet correlations in pp and Pb+Pb collisions at $$ \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.02 $$ TeV with the ATLAS detector

Carleton physicists Dag Gillberg, Jesse Heilman, Alain Bellerive, Thomas Koffas and Manuella Vincter are authors on this ATLAS collaboration paper, which measured how J/ψ particles are produced alongside jets in proton-proton and lead-lead collisions at the Large Hadron Collider. J/ψ production is strongly suppressed in lead-lead collisions up to the highest momentum measured, with differences between J/ψ particles that are and are not near jets, and the results constrain how these particles are produced.

Journal of High Energy Physics · Nov 4 · open access · large collaboration
